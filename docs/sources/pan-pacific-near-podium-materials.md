The initial IOR 1.5 candidate had a detailed, unobstructed native cube capture but nearly black head-on panes. A controlled local change to effective IOR 1.8 in both optical passes increases normal Fresnel reflectance from 4% to approximately 8.16%, with about 4.3% less normal transmitted backing contribution. All other material and probe settings stayed fixed. This is an authored approximation for multiple glazing interfaces, not measured bulk IOR or evidence of a particular coating.

Matched close and wide native comparisons showed modest additional blue/sky variation while retaining dark glazing and physical recess depth, without a new mirror-like sheen or glare. The adjustment was accepted. Reflected-city detail remains limited. ## Fine mineral panel joints — 2026-11-16 candidate

The accepted `near-podium-fresnel-close.png` still shows broad opaque fields as uninterrupted mineral sheets. Handford20 shows aligned vertical and horizontal panel joints on both the curved pale spine and the opaque fields between openings. The local original-pixel crop `artifacts/local/near-podium-panel-reference-crop.png` covers x315–605/y960–1305. Vertical joints continue through several spandrels; courses bend in perspective across the spine. These are fine panel divisions, not thick brick mortar. Several joints are obscured by reflections, vegetation and the bridge.

The fitted schedule is **0.8 m wide × 0.9 m high**, a **6 mm joint** and **1.5 mm shallow recess response**. This is an estimate from the roughly 30–35 pixel course spacing over the registered approximately 7 m tall lower field, and a few narrow panels across the curved return. The photograph is not a survey or a material sample. Panel widths and alignment around hidden returns remain inferred. The implementation uses aligned courses with 14% darkening only at resolved seams, rather than introducing a general darkening, weathering or fake shadow band.

`pan-pacific-near-podium-joints.ts` adds the finish only to the concrete material, after cloning the soffit. The three existing licensed mineral maps, their sampler transforms, normal strength and PBR lighting remain. Horizontal surfaces in that same batch are excluded by their geometric normals; the separate soffit, frame, backing and glass materials have no new shader. The relief perturbs the lit normal after the mineral normal map and never displaces geometry or changes shadow silhouettes, collision, support or openings. Pixel-derivative filtering reduces subpixel joint contrast at distance. No new texture, photograph reuse, material cohort, light or capture is added.

The old metre UV generator derives each facet's horizontal axis from that facet's normal. Across the 24-facet spine this creates large phase changes at duplicated vertices, so stamping a grid into those UVs would visibly reset at every facet. The panel finish instead projects world position onto the 15 registered source runs (five edges at each tier). Cumulative run length and the analytic arc length of the actual parabolic spine provide a continuous horizontal coordinate. Vertical coordinates use the shared PIER floor datum. The mineral map UVs remain byte-for-byte unchanged. The calculation is bounded to this one premerged concrete mesh and adds no per-panel draws; the full near-return assembly remains four opaque draws plus the two existing optical passes.
