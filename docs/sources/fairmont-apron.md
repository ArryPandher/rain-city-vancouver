# Fairmont Waterfront driveway and exterior apron

The Fairmont Howe frontage had an unsupported strip at the old world ground datum, separated from the raised Howe road by the edge of the Canada Place support mask. This was not evidence of an intentionally lower hotel entrance. The previous implementation explicitly excluded several service ways from the raised road system while their exact levels remained unresolved.

## Source registration and evidence limits

The cached OpenStreetMap extract `artifacts/local/canada-approach-buildings-reference/osm-map.xml` identifies **w251586099** as `highway=service`, `service=driveway`, `oneway=yes`, with no `layer`, `tunnel` or `bridge` tag. Its north endpoint shares node **2405683342** with Canada Place way **w1255990035**; its south endpoint shares node **988212032** with Howe ways **w232195213 / w1255990033**. The runtime source polyline is 61.297 m long and runs from **(475.6, −502.8)** to **(478.4, −443.5)**. © OpenStreetMap contributors, ODbL 1.0. These tags establish connected topology, not surveyed elevation.

Before correction, this driveway's `roadHeight` returned zero even at the shared junctions, where the actual Canada Place site returned **7.73 m** and **3.693158668 m** respectively. Between the hotel wall and existing Howe support, the unfilled gap was approximately 4.65–24.15 m wide in sampled cross-sections. For example, at hotel wall **(469.59, −474.83)**, the first original Howe support was near **(493.70, −474.83)** at **6.547 m**. At **(468.34, −453.13)**, it began near **(472.77, −451.89)** at **3.86 m**. A zero-height service ribbon at these junctions was therefore an implementation mismatch.

The existing MCM hotel photograph supports an exterior vehicle court, substantial round piers and a canopy above a continuous shaded frontage. It does not establish this reconstructed street gradient. The historical City roof record **162733** supplies roof elevation **22.08 m**, local base **15.65 m** and height above ground **6.43 m**. These absolute City elevations are not world Y coordinates. The corresponding canopy geometry uses the relative 6.43 m anchor with an explicitly estimated grade adaptation; a fixed world roof Y of 8.12 m would be buried by a continuous raised driveway. See [the hotel source record](fairmont-waterfront.md) for the separate facade and canopy interpretation.

## Bounded shared surface

`fairmont-apron-site.ts` creates an exterior polygon bounded by the mapped Howe/Canada Place road centrelines, the two shared driveway junctions and the original hotel's Howe boundary. It clips every building footprint out of that polygon. Its source extent is approximately **x465.05–503.5, z−502.8–−443.5**. The surrounding Canada Place support remains authoritative wherever it already exists; building centroids and space outside the bounded addition keep their previous baseline.

The new-area field blends cached samples of the actual original support edge toward an estimated driveway-station profile anchored at the two existing junction heights. Distance-based weighting avoids winner-switch discontinuities at medial lines. A short transition at the original edge preserves its height and slope; a corner allowance prevents the hotel's south tip from pinching incompatible target levels into a narrow ridge. The direct endpoint-average profile is approximately **6.59%**, but the final edge-matched driveway is steeper in parts. Dense route checks bound its maximum at **under 16%**, with no abrupt height steps. This is an estimated urban driveway grade, not a surveyed gradient or an accessibility claim. Ordinary vehicle handling and the complete pavement context still require native gameplay review.

The earlier unmasked Howe/Canada height field must not simply be extrapolated across the gap: it produced a false approximately 36% driveway slope near the road's medial transition. The apron uses actual boundary samples instead. The remaining service ways **w143682532, w143682533 and w251863020** retain their previous policy; only the source-confirmed Fairmont driveway leaves the zero-service exception.

`createCanadaPlaceSite` unions this apron with the original support polygons, rather than rendering a duplicate overlaid slab. Its `contains`, `supportHeight`, `baselineHeight` and `roadIds` now include the driveway and apron. The separate PwC apron is constructed against the same frozen original support, not against a chained Fairmont height field. The renderer and collision therefore receive the same finished site geometry. `covers` includes a **0.1 mm** boundary tolerance for Float32-rendered vertices while rejecting distant queries before polygon scans; strict `contains` retains the actual footprint test.

## Verification and remaining integration

Focused tests check both exact junction heights, continuous driveway samples every 5 cm, original-support and outside-scope invariance, hotel contact samples, thousands of 1 mm continuity probes, excluded building interiors, one unioned support surface and matching heights at polygon boundaries. Existing Canada Place site tests retain the western tie, entry datum, unrelated areas and inside-corner behavior.

The approach renderer now subdivides by surface-height interpolation error as well as edge length. Its boundary normals sample the same owning apron field on both sides of the derivative, avoiding a false lighting cliff caused by falling back to an unrelated unmasked road profile. Float32 boundary vertices use the tolerance above. All five focused renderer checks pass with their existing limits: less than 3 cm centroid interpolation error, upward normal Y above 0.8, fewer than 60,000 triangles, at most four batches and unchanged building/deck exclusions. Native vehicle handling, visual acceptance and performance are recorded separately in [the scene report](../HOWE-STREET-SCENE.md).
