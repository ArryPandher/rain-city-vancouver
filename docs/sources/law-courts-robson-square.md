# Law Courts and Robson Square references

Original game geometry is guided by these published architectural references:

- [Arthur Erickson — Law Courts Complex](https://www.arthurerickson.com/government-buildings/law-courts-complex/), including the aerial photograph by Ezra Stoller / ESTO. The official description identifies the large sloping glass roof and space truss. Designed 1973.
- [Terri Meyer Boake, University of Waterloo — Law Courts image gallery](https://www.tboake.com/lawcourts.html): concrete structure, recessed galleries, exposed hollow-steel roof frame, front/rear elevations and terrace details.
- [Vancouver Heritage Foundation — Robson Square](https://placesthatmatter.ca/location/robson-square/): three-block civic landscape, glass courthouse canopy, landscaped terraces, pools and waterfalls.
- [The Cultural Landscape Foundation — Robson Square](https://www.tclf.org/robson-square): continuous roof garden designed by Cornelia Hahn Oberlander and Arthur Erickson, linking the Law Courts to the Art Gallery.

Reference photographs are studied locally and are not distributed in the game. The game uses original modeled geometry, existing CC0 Concrete Wall 009 photography by Charlotte Baglioni / Poly Haven, existing licensed foliage photography, and original glass/steel/water materials. No reference photograph is used as a game texture.

The Law Courts replaces OSM building `w137903324` within its mapped footprint. Its asymmetric glazed canopy, triangulated double-layer frame, projecting concrete slabs, recessed glazing, courtroom masses and terrace pool are visual approximations. The roof span (81 m in the authored model), 34.6 m crown, bay spacing and intermediate floor heights are inferred, not surveyed. Interior masses are visible through the canopy; the courthouse itself remains a collision shell and is not enterable.

The office footprint `w1384440657` under Robson Square is represented as a public roof garden rather than a solid box. Its original outline remains, with a continuous, simplified elevation profile up to 6.48 m. Rendering, walking, cycling and driving share that height function. Perimeter transitions return to grade. Planted beds and the pool block travel; the main routes and two small glazed entry canopies are open. Fifteen authored garden trees supplement the mapped trees. Pools, benches, planting, ramps and canopy shapes are approximate; the actual complex has finer stair, waterfall and underground-rink geometry that this version does not reproduce.

## Gallery-facing lower stair (2026-11-14)

The TCLF description explicitly identifies the upper gardens' stair-ramps (“stramps”), concrete/pink-granite paving and the route returning to grade in front of the Art Gallery. Its [2019 Charles A. Birnbaum photograph 060](https://www.tclf.org/sites/default/files/styles/full_width/public/thumbnails/image/CANADA_BritishColumbia_Vancouver_RobsonSquare_byCharlesABirnbaum_2019_060_sig_006.jpg) shows broad concrete steps in the gallery-facing terrace sequence, with the gallery dome and portico beyond the planted landscape. Read from the public page and inspected locally; this photograph is not bundled or used as a texture. The existing Art Gallery GLB was inspected: 25,193 triangles, five materials (foundation/glass/stone/copper/trim); its historic facade remains unchanged.

The previous garden interpreted every terrace transition as a smooth ramp. One lower transition now has an explicit, broad concrete stair, descending along the established garden/gallery axis (increasing local u), while the parallel continuous route at v = −22 remains intact. The run is inset within the mapped garden footprint: u −190…−178 and v −18…−6. Ten 16cm risers and 1.2m treads descend from 1.68m to 0.08m. These dimensions and exact placement adapt the reference motif to the existing authored levels; they are **not surveyed stramp coordinates**, a reconstruction of the original diagonal ramp geometry, or a code-accessibility claim.

The upgrade adds 60 triangles with top, riser and triangular cheek faces, batched into the existing terrace-concrete material. There are no new textures, trees, lighting, solid walls, or per-step draw calls. The old ramp remains as the underlying continuous substrate; every tread sits no more than 16cm above it and the shared collision height now follows the treads. The side route preserves its prior continuous slope. Gallery/courthouse collision, pool/planter footprints and canopy passage remain unchanged.

Tests raycast all ten treads at several positions, compare their exact heights to collision, check the small substrate/step separation, and verify the parallel route remains traversable. Existing garden collision and substrate-error tests still pass. Native daylight appearance remains for the parent session to verify.

Inspection route: fast travel **Robson Square**, walk toward world (−131.52, 114.55), the lower stair approach. Look uphill at heading −0.785 radians; walk the flight toward (−145.66,128.69). From that upper point, heading 2.356 looks down the steps toward the gallery. The continuous side route is approximately four metres to the v-negative side, at v = −22.

## Art Gallery rear entrance placement repair

The subsequent native daylight view from the upper Robson stair showed the gallery as plain massing with pitched side roofs. Audit of the actual quantized GLB and converted downtown OSM polygons found only `w69701736` intersecting the gallery hull; its centroid lies inside the landmark suppression hull. There was no overlapping generic gallery shell in this audit. The gabled side wings belong to the source asset and were retained.

A concrete source error was identified in [geometry.js](https://github.com/codriver-io/codriver-3d-assets/blob/main/src/peregrine/landmarks/top-cities/vancouver-art-gallery/geometry.js): its rear engaged columns/cornice/pediment and stairs were authored near local v=12.17, while the corresponding mapped south wall in [the source site data](https://github.com/codriver-io/codriver-3d-assets/blob/main/src/peregrine/landmarks/top-cities/vancouver-art-gallery/vancouver-art-gallery-site.js) lies near v=23.52–23.82. A ray from the actual Robson viewing position hit the source stone wall roughly11m before the intended rear column. The large four-column front portico is correctly on the opposite Georgia side; it was not moved.

The dedicated repair translates **only930 reviewed vertices** of the existing rear entrance by **11.33m** along source v. This places the column centres at v=23.50, engaged into the real rear wall with their bases overlapping the foundation; the cornice and pediment project in front. The 12-step flight begins at v=23.43 and reaches v=27.63, overlapping the facade at its upper end instead of leaving a gap. Geometry height, proportions, all25,193 triangles, five materials, dome, front portico, wall/window vertices and side roofs remain unchanged. Source attribution/license remain Codriver CC-BY-4.0 and OpenStreetMap geography.

Reproduce the reviewed vertex ranges with `node scripts/prepare-gallery-rear-entry.mjs`. The script decodes the actual quantized GLB, identifies connected source components by the kit's rear-entry dimensions, and writes explicit vertex ranges plus component bounds and source SHA256 in `src/world/gallery-rear-entry-data.json`. Runtime moves these exact ranges, not a spatial-box selection. Tests pin the source hash and prove all unselected vertices and all indices unchanged. The original GLB is not overwritten.

A matching bounded step-height function now supports the newly exposed exterior treads. It is enabled only when the gallery's mapped building exists. Tests compare36 actual quantized tread ray hits with collision within5mm, verify the outside steps are open, retain the closed historic facade, and show the rear column hits before its former occluding wall. No interior is opened and no global terrain is changed.

Native inspection target: stand at world (−55.78,34.43), face heading2.365 radians toward the rear entrance. The flight runs from approximately(−54.10,32.72) toward(−51.23,29.79). The same upper Robson viewing pose (−146.56,1.68,127.88) remains useful to compare the previously buried rear entrance. The correction does not establish photographic fidelity for the whole gallery; remaining source simplification and daylight material appearance require native review.

Native follow-up exposed an intersecting mapped footway ribbon draping over the repaired stairs after collision gained their height. Generic pedestrian ribbons are now clipped triangle-by-triangle against only the custom stair rectangle, with a1.5cm collar for quantized source edges. Both approach portions remain; newly exposed vertices re-evaluate ground height so an old elevated endpoint cannot leave a sloping strip outside the cut. Streets and remote paths are untouched. Regression tests raycast all twelve treads against the actual nearby mapped footways and verify no generic overlay remains; synthetic crossing tests verify both approaches remain at their ground datum. The step-height/collision tests continue to pass.
