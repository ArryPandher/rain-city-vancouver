They support the architectural type; the following cavity dimensions and pane subdivision spacing are explicitly authored approximations, not surveyed construction details or a depiction of the actual interior.

Existing longitudinal mullions now use a dedicated pale upper-frame material, while intermediate rails divide each quarter-round into four bands (approximately 0.9 m arc length). Ground door materials and geometry remain separate.

A closed structural backing sits 400 mm inward along the pane's analytic normal. Dark muted backing faces and pale side reveals provide real view-dependent separation behind the material agent's thin-sheet transparent glass. Reveals close each longitudinal bay and the ends of each curved tier; tessellation inside the tier introduces no extra opaque crossbars. Identical adjoining reveal surfaces are deduplicated. The backing is a shallow closed visual proxy, not a furnished or accessible concourse. Exact cavity depth, backing colour and structure are estimates subject to native visual calibration.

Source selection still removes exactly 10 reviewed concrete,100 glass and 480 trim triangles. Existing roof/back/transition closures stay intact. The ground-door fallback stays opaque, and any explicitly supplied ground-door material is retained.

Total returned facade geometry: 61084 triangles in 8 material batches, including 9560 backing/reveal triangles in one batch. Splitting opaque end caps adds the second additional batch over the former 6. The existing guard is unchanged and remains a separate mesh. There are no added textures, reflection captures, playable interiors or collision/route changes.

Seventeen targeted facade/guard/material tests pass, plus TypeScript. Tests retain actual decoded source GLB geometry and verify normal and both oblique rays behind transparent panes first meet the new shallow backing, not residual source walls. Existing exterior curvature, roof/guard contact, source selection, source paving height and public-route tests remain passing. Final transparency/depth appearance requires the parent's native browser comparison; these checks do not establish photographic parity.

The 400 mm closed depth and exterior coordinates are unchanged. Both portions are created after deduplicating the complete shared return, preserving one physical wall between adjacent bays. A targeted geometry test casts rays at six depths across the split, verifies pale vertices stop at 100 mm, confirms uninterrupted coverage, and checks that shared bays do not duplicate either colour band. Splitting the returns adds 3428 triangles without additional materials or draws.

## Closed concourse volume revision

The earlier 0.40 m curved backing was a visual approximation and is superseded. Rayner's west section depicts open concourse volume behind the stepped envelope, not a close concentric opaque lining. SACO's west overview and the official Canada Place 2011 west photograph independently reveal depth behind the panes. Their night illumination is not used as an emissive or daylight colour specification.

The helper now preserves only the 0.10 m pale perimeter rebates. It closes the visible volume at the existing source hall-back plane, source floor 7.8 m, roof 17.65 m, existing upright fascias and both existing end profiles. Inner lining faces sit 0.025 m inside those bounds to avoid coplanar source surfaces. Station bends and taper come from the existing source-registered data. Roof reach follows the current opaque/glazed longitudinal layout. Floor remains at concourse grade, never at the 10.75 m door head. These are interior-facing closure surfaces for an exterior view, not a playable interior or a surveyed fit-out.

No furniture, invented room partitions, random panel colour variation or night-derived emission is added. Existing pane ribs remain; additional diagonal-member spacing has not been established well enough to reproduce safely. The untextured muted floor/back and lighter ceiling are neutral authored estimates. Large-scale interior composition and material finish remain incomplete, but several metres of actual view depth replace the false uniformly close sheet.

Dedicated ray tests verify deep closure, floor/roof datum, finite geometry and retained shallow rebates without opaque pane-sized back sheets. Whole-scene first-hit tests must allow the existing source geometry or lining to close a ray at their real depth; the former sub-metre backing expectation is obsolete. Native visibility and performance still require review.

The lining floor follows `solidBayOutward` within full-height opaque intervals and `groundOutward` elsewhere, with a 0.025 m inward edge inset. Existing station cuts include all four solid-bay boundaries (19, 33, 70, 83 m). Recessed public walkway strips therefore receive no raised interior lining. Dedicated downward ray tests cover every solid-bay station interval, confirming floor inside and no lining outside the recessed footprint. Existing opaque bay fronts, dark round-opening infill, source back/roof and facade lateral closure meshes are retained; this helper adds no replacement exterior bay faces.

A subsequent two-sided visibility audit found that exterior FrontSide faces alone did not close the volume when viewed through adjacent glazing. The helper now adds inner linings for all four exact source-profile returns, offset 0.025 m toward the adjacent glazed volume, and solid front linings 0.32 m inward (behind the original 0.28 m circular infill). These are inside faces of existing walls, not new partitions or altered outside geometry. The return contour reproduces the owner's profile/ranges, including its clamped upper depth and adjacent fascia state. Integrated rays verify all four returns at three heights, both solid fronts from inside, and the original circular infill remaining the first exterior hit. This supersedes the earlier assumption that retaining one-sided owner meshes was sufficient.

The exterior-first-hit regression test additionally establishes the side of each return: beyond the recessed solid front, the solid interval is exterior walkway, while the neighboring glass interval contains the concourse. Return linings therefore offset into the glass interval, preserving the original wall as the first hit from the public recess.
