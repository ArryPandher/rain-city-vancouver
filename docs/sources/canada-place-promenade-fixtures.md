# Canada Place promenade fixtures

Original exterior model guided by the cached [TourbyTransit west-trail photograph](https://cdn.tourbytransit.com/vancouver/images/Canadian-Trail-at-Canada-Place.jpg) and [Brenco's 2023 entrance photograph](https://www.brenco.com/images/whats-happening/Salish-Sea--the-Journey-Within-1678831506.jpg), from its [project article](https://www.brenco.com/whats-happening/salish-sea-the-journey-within). These establish a pale fixture family and banner-bearing public realm. TourbyTransit is historical (old entrance arch); Brenco corroborates the public setting but does not prove every historical head or banner remained identical. No reference photograph is distributed as a runtime asset.

The model uses one clear round globe, visible non-emissive lamp/socket/reflector, a pale stepped pole with a short upper crank, connection collars, attached banner bars, and a subtly bowed fabric panel. Distant apparent paired heads are not sufficient to mandate a double-head assembly at every location; this is explicitly a bounded single-head interpretation. Exact fixture dimensions are estimates, not a survey: height 4.62 m, globe diameter 0.54 m, base radius 0.16 m and conservative collision radius 0.18 m. Banner lower edge is 2.15 m above the deck, width 0.85 m and height 1.65 m.

The WATERFRONT and CANADA PLACE banner graphic is original Canvas2D artwork with antialiased medium-weight sans-serif lettering at 512 × 1024 pixels. It is not a copy of temporary event artwork or a claim that this exact sign exists at these positions. Warm coral, sand and navy fields with original shoreline curves supply a restrained colorful vertical banner without imported imagery or font assets. One texture is shared by the row. Node geometry tests can inject a texture factory; environments without Canvas2D receive a solid coral fallback.

`addCanadaPlacePromenadeFixtures(parent, placements)` accepts `{x,z,yaw,groundY}` (optional legacy `y`) in source-local metres. Ground defaults to 7.8 m. Model +X follows the row; +Z points inland. Placement and path-clearance data are authored separately. The returned group uses five shared InstancedMesh batches for all fixtures: pale metal, socket hardware, glass, cloth and unlit lamp elements. There are no point lights, emissive materials, runtime animation or extra reflection captures. Glass casting is disabled; opaque parts cast shadows. Several transparent instances are sorted as one object, so inspect overlapping globes from along-row views before acceptance.

Tests cover finite metric bounds, grounded geometry, low-level collision radius, banner head clearance, non-emission, shared instancing and placement transforms. Native walking-distance review still decides silhouette, banner legibility, transparency and joins; these tests do not establish photographic quality.

## Seaward banner orientation

The nearest TourbyTransit banner presents its broad face to approaching northbound walkers and extends to the water side of its pole. Its apparent width relative to the nearby globe is consistent with the estimated 0.85 m banner and 0.54 m globe. The model therefore places cloth across the row, spanning local Z −0.10 to −0.95 m, rather than along local X. Its spine remains near X 0.10 m. Pole, globe and crank are unchanged; the upper bracket connects back to the jogged stem at X 0.20 m, while the lower bracket connects to the straight stem. Seaward projection above 2.15 m is intentional; placement/camera envelopes are verified separately.

Both printed faces use the same original texture, opposite winding and mirrored U coordinates, with 2 mm separation. This keeps lettering readable from either walking direction without a second material draw. Model rays verify front/back normals and UV relation, non-coplanarity, seaward bounds and the physical bracket joins. Dimensions, historical fixture identity and placement remain bounded estimates.
