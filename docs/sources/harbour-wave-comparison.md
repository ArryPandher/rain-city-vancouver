## Observed issue and reference limits

`artifacts/local/water-north-horizon-revision-native.png` still shows conspicuous repeating parallel water bands after the HDR horizon continuation removed the original color fallback. The original normal field has two world-X/Z sinusoids, wavelengths 8.73 m and 6.83 m, plus axis-aligned value-noise grids at 0.5 m and 5.56 m. Their phase speeds are 1.25 and 0.76 m/s. Its central differences are not divided by twice the 0.15 m sample step; the normal's 0.65 vertical component makes the resulting slopes approximately 0.4615 times the mathematical derivative. These are authored animation/scaling choices, not a measured harbour spectrum.

The retained [Graham Ironworkers improvements photograph](https://www.grahambuilds.com/ironworkers-memorial-bridge-improvements/), `artifacts/local/ironworkers-reference/graham-improvements.jpg`, shows short uneven wind ripples beside the marine yard, with coherent reflections of sheds and pilings. No new reference download or runtime photo asset is used.

## One independent, debug-only candidate

`water-waves.ts` replaces only the normal calculation when its separate day-only debug control is enabled. Eight directional components share a broad authored wind sector (6°..68° from world +X toward +Z) and different phases. Wavelengths are 11.7, 8.1, 5.6, 3.8, 2.5, 1.6, 0.95 and 0.55 metres. This wind sector is a plausible test arrangement, not a current Vancouver wind observation. No component follows a world axis, and the field is world-registered rather than camera-aligned.

Each height component is `A*sin(k·p − omega*t + phase)`, with unit propagation direction, wave number `2*pi/wavelength`, and deep-water gravity dispersion `omega²=9.81*k`. Periods range 2.74..0.594 seconds. Analytic slopes use `A*k*cos(phase)`, with metre heights and dimensionless slopes; there is no finite-difference scale fudge. This is a small-amplitude deep-water approximation, not a shoreline refraction, wake or current simulation.

The authored spectrum has RMS height 0.0485 m, sum-of-amplitudes height bound 0.1619 m, RMS slope 0.0660 and worst-case slope bound 0.259. Those describe the virtual height field used for normals; **the mesh remains flat** at the existing water datum and collision/contact is unchanged. There is no crest displacement, foam generation, fetch model, vessel wake or invented shoreline bathymetry.

For each component, screen derivatives of world XZ give its largest X/Y phase increment per pixel. The amplitude fades smoothly from fully resolved at four pixels per wavelength to zero at two pixels, using `1-smoothstep(pi/2,pi,phaseFootprint)`. This applies before normal construction and follows the actual grazing screen footprint rather than a fixed distance cutoff. Unresolved waves do not retain aliasing contrast. The cutoff is a conservative screen filter, not an exact optical integration.

HDR roughness deliberately remains unchanged for this independent A/B. Unresolved slope variance is **not** converted into microfacet roughness in this pass; distant water can become too smooth or glossy. That limitation must be assessed natively rather than concealed with color/noise or exposure changes. The original additive colored-noise term also remains untouched, so not every distant repetitive artifact is promised to disappear.

## Ownership, cost and validation

`World.setDebugWaterWaves(enabled): boolean` requires the existing `debug` URL flag to enable. The visible control is **Directional harbour waves (day only)**; root owns its main.ts checkbox and `lighting.waterWaves` profile hookup. `World.waterWavesDiagnostics()` reports activation, eight components, filter description, unchanged HDR roughness and zero added textures/draws. World weather updates the uniform; disabling restores the original compile hook/cache key. Material disposal releases the hook.

The scoped `onBeforeCompile` hook runs after the existing base/HDR shader selection. It does not edit the base material's shader text or own the HDR target. Both HDR/wave toggle orders are covered, including disabling HDR while retaining waves, re-enabling it, then disabling waves while retaining the same new HDR target. Original compile hooks and cache-key functions are restored by identity, and changes request recompilation. No renderer-private patch or new capture is used.

The active normal computation uses eight cosine terms and eight derivative-based attenuation evaluations. The old normal path evaluates four samples of two sinusoids plus two four-corner hash-noise fields: approximately 40 sine/hash trigonometric evaluations, excluding the additive color noise that both retain. This is a source operation count, not measured GPU speed; shader compilers and divergent workload can change actual performance. There are no extra vertex attributes, draw calls, geometry, textures or per-frame allocations.

Eleven focused wave/optics tests and full TypeScript checking pass. Analytic slopes agree with independent numerical differentiation at Canada Place, Ironworkers and large time values; tests check gravity dispersion, bounded height/slope, both screen-axis filters, unchanged optical terms, shader guards, toggle permutations, material cleanup and the non-debug World gate. No browser/GPU shader compilation, build or default promotion was performed for this candidate.

For native review, use the same north public promenade point `[686.70,7.88,-697.34]` and recorded camera `[684.6433,10.5121,-693.5631]`, yaw 2.6432963, pitch 0.19. First hold HDR enabled and toggle only waves; then verify original reflection with waves independently. Keep sun, weather and exposure fixed. Check short broken crests near the railing, coherent larger wind ripples farther out, stable grazing pixels during a small pan, and whether filtered distant water becomes excessively smooth. Inspect day/night restoration and toggle order. Repeating additive-noise patterns, absent actual shore/ship reflections and calm-water assumptions remain separate issues.
